SECTION 1: ZSTC GRADING STANDARDS
================================================================

--- 1.1 GRADE 1 (PREMIUM) ---
Grade 1 is the highest quality whole clove for export.
- Color: Attractive saffron / golden color (well-matured)
- Moisture Content: Maximum 14%
- Shape: Uniform, with heads intact, derived from well-matured buds
- Mpeta (Khoker/Grade 4 cloves): Must NOT exceed 3%
- Foreign Matter (stems, seeds, matende, debris): Must NOT exceed 5%
- Free from mold
- ZSTC 2023 Price: TZS 15,000 per kg

--- 1.2 GRADE 2 ---
Grade 2 is similar to Grade 1 but with diminished visual appeal.
- Color: Slightly blackish or faded (not as attractive as Grade 1)
- Moisture Content: Maximum 14%
- Mpeta: Must NOT exceed 7%
- Foreign Matter (stems, matende, other debris): Must NOT exceed 5%
- ZSTC 2023 Price: TZS 13,500 per kg
- Revenue loss vs Grade 1: 10% (TZS 1,500/kg)

--- 1.3 GRADE 3 ---
Grade 3 cloves have more significantly faded color.
- Color: More faded than Grade 1 and Grade 2
- Dryness: Must pass a brittleness/break test (snap test)
- Mpeta: Must NOT exceed 20%
- Foreign Matter (leaves, stems, matende): Must NOT exceed 5%
- Characteristic: Large quantity of headless cloves present
- ZSTC 2023 Price: TZS 12,500 per kg
- Revenue loss vs Grade 1: 16.7% (TZS 2,500/kg)

--- 1.4 GRADE 4 — MPETA (KHOKER CLOVES) ---
Grade 4 consists primarily of Mpeta, locally known as Khoker cloves.
- These are fermented, poorly dried, or otherwise defective cloves
- Mpeta content: Greater than 20%
- Badli dried cloves (pale brown, fermented during drying) are classified here
- ZSTC 2023 Price: TZS 7,000 per kg
- Revenue loss vs Grade 1: 53.3% (TZS 8,000/kg)

--- 1.5 GRADING QUICK-REFERENCE TABLE ---
Parameter        | Grade 1   | Grade 2        | Grade 3        | Grade 4
-----------------+-----------+----------------+----------------+------------------
Color            | Golden    | Faded/Blackish | More Faded     | Primarily Mpeta
Moisture (max)   | 14%       | 14%            | Brittleness test| N/A
Foreign Matter   | ≤5%       | ≤5%            | ≤5%            | N/A
Mpeta Content    | ≤3%       | ≤7%            | ≤20%           | >20%
Price (TZS/kg)   | 15,000    | 13,500         | 12,500         | 7,000

--- 1.6 ECONOMIC IMPACT OF MISGRADING ---
Misclassifying Grade 1 as Grade 2 = 10% revenue loss per batch.
Misclassifying Grade 3 as Grade 4 = 44% revenue loss per batch.
Misclassifying Grade 1 as Grade 4 = 53.3% revenue loss per batch.
Accurate grading is therefore critical to farmer income. ZSTC uses a bulk
rule-based approach (not single-clove analysis) when buying from farmers.

--- 1.7 WHAT IS MPETA (KHOKER)? ---
Mpeta is the local Swahili/Zanzibar term for what international trade calls
"khoker" cloves. These are cloves that are:
- Poorly dried, causing fermentation
- Pale brown or discolored (not golden or dark brown)
- Often soft rather than brittle
- Produced when drying is rushed or done during rain
The percentage of Mpeta in a batch is the single most decisive grading factor.

SECTION 2: CLOVE AGRONOMY — CULTIVATION GUIDE
================================================================

--- 2.1 BOTANICAL IDENTITY ---
Scientific Name: Syzygium aromaticum
Family: Myrtaceae
Type: Evergreen tree, aromatic dried flower bud
Native to: Indonesia and India; widely grown in Zanzibar, Pemba, Madagascar

--- 2.2 CLIMATE REQUIREMENTS ---
- Rainfall: 150 to 250 cm per year, well distributed
- Temperature: Warm humid tropical
- Altitude: Sea level up to 800–900 meters
- Shade: Clove plants prefer partial shade, especially when young

--- 2.3 SOIL REQUIREMENTS ---
- Best soil: Rich loamy soils with good drainage
- Also grows in: Heavier red soils (with drainage)
- Critical requirement: Good drainage — waterlogging causes root rot and
  promotes Sudden Death disease
- Soil in Zanzibar and Pemba is well-suited for clove cultivation

--- 2.4 PROPAGATION ---
- Main method: By seed (commercially preferred)
- Seeds: Collected from fully developed fruits of productive mother trees
- Pre-sowing: Soak seeds before sowing; sow immediately after harvest
- Germination: 10 to 15 days; seedlings are ready for nursery in ~45 days

--- 2.5 NURSERY BED PREPARATION ---
- Bed dimensions: 15–20 cm height, 1 m width, convenient length
- Top layer: 5–8 cm of sand
- Seed spacing: 2 cm apart
- Shade: Provide partial shade to protect from direct sunlight
- Polybag method: Use 30x15 cm polybags filled with sand:soil:organic
  matter (cow dung) in ratio 3:3:1

--- 2.6 PLANTING IN THE FIELD ---
- Best planting time: Onset of South-West monsoon (June–July)
  In low-lying areas: Towards end of monsoons
- Pit size: 75 cm × 75 cm × 75 cm
- Spacing: 6 to 7 meters between trees
  (Intercrop spacing: based on the main/major crop spacing)
- Pit preparation: Partially fill with cattle manure, compost, or green
  leaves, then cover with topsoil before planting
- Intercropping: Cloves grow well mixed with coconut, coffee, areca nut

--- 2.7 FERTILIZERS AND MANURE ---
Young trees (annual):
- Organic: 50–60 kg compost/manure + bone meal or fish meal per tree/year
- Apply in May–June (onset of rains), in trenches around the tree
- Inorganic: 20g N (430g urea) + 18g P2O5 (110g superphosphate) +
  50g K2O (80g Muriate of potash) per tree per year

Mature trees (15+ years):
- 300g N (600g urea) + 250g P2O5 (1,560g superphosphate) +
  750g K2O (1,250g Muriate of potash) per tree per year
- Apply in 2 equal split doses: May–June and September–October
- Trench location: 1 to 1.5 meters from the base of the tree
- Always mulch the plant basin and keep it weed-free

--- 2.8 WATER MANAGEMENT ---
- Watering is essential for the first 3–4 years
- Pot/drip watering recommended during establishment
- Adult trees can survive drought but water them for better yield/quality
- Extreme care needed to maintain minimum soil moisture in dry months
- NOTE: Waterlogging is dangerous — it promotes root rot and Sudden Death

--- 2.9 TRAINING AND PRUNING (COPPICING) ---
- Coppicing: Cutting back tree height for manageable commercial plantation
- Timing: June–July when tree is 2–3 years old
- Cut to: ~15 cm from the stump
- Result: 2–3 side shoots form; tree becomes a low bush ~2 m high
  within 4 years; suitable for regular harvesting
- Regular peeling operations begin from 4th–5th year
- Coppicing is typically done in alternate years

--- 2.10 WEED CONTROL ---
- Manual weeding by removing/cutting overcrowded branches
- Apply 1% Bordeaux mixture to prevent dieback

--- 2.11 YIELD EXPECTATIONS (Tanzania/Zanzibar context) ---
- Tree age 3–5 years: 10–20 kg dried cloves per tree per year
- Tree age 8–10 years: 40–80 kg per year
- Tree age 15+ years: 100–130 kg per year
- Full bearing stage: reached after ~15 years
- Flowering begins: From 4th year under good conditions
- Harvest seasons: April–May and August–October (two seasons/year)

SECTION 3: HARVESTING
================================================================

--- 3.1 WHEN TO HARVEST ---
- Harvest when buds turn from green/olive to yellow-pink (before petals open)
- Buds at correct stage: Less than 2 cm long
- Opened flowers have NO commercial value as a spice
- Flowering season: September–October (plains); December–January (high altitude)

--- 3.2 FIRST COMMERCIAL HARVEST ---
- First harvest: When tree is 6–8 years old
- Full production: After 15 years

--- 3.3 HOW TO HARVEST ---
- Hand-pick clusters of flower buds from branches
- Use step ladders; DO NOT damage branches (damages future crops)
- Detach buds from stalks: hold cluster in one hand, press against palm of
  other hand, and slowly twist so buds fall off
- Keep hands and workspace very clean to avoid contamination

================================================================
SECTION 4: POST-HARVEST HANDLING AND DRYING
================================================================

--- 4.1 PRE-TREATMENT ---
- Separate buds from stalks immediately after harvest
- Stems and buds are dried separately (stems can be used for oil distillation)
- Maintain cleanliness throughout to prevent contamination

--- 4.2 DRYING PROCESS (CRITICAL FOR GRADING) ---
- Spread buds on clean mats in the sun immediately; DO NOT delay (risk of fermentation)
- Rake and turn frequently for even drying and even brown color
- Color progression: pale russet → golden brown → dark brown (fully dried)
- Duration: 4–5 days
- Do NOT speed up drying — cloves will become dry, brittle, and withered
  rather than plump (reducing quality to Grade 3 or Grade 4)
- Final target moisture content: 8–10% (field standard)
- ZSTC buying standard: 14% maximum moisture

CRITICAL NOTE: Cloves must be dried quickly after harvest. Any delay causes fermentation,
producing Mpeta (Khoker/Grade 4) — the most financially damaging outcome.

--- 4.3 DRYING TEST ---
- Brittleness/Snap test: Fully dried cloves snap (break) easily
- If cloves bend rather than snap: not yet dry enough
- Well dried: 1 kg of dried cloves = approximately 3 kg of fresh cloves
- 11,000 to 15,000 dried buds = 1 kg of product

--- 4.4 RAINY SEASON DRYING ---
- During rainy season: Use mechanical dryers (e.g., tray dryer)
- Sun drying during rain leads to fungal growth, fermentation, and
  downgrading to Grade 3 or Mpeta (Grade 4)
- ZSTC grades heavily penalize rain-damaged, pale, and fermented cloves

--- 4.5 WINNOWING ---
- After drying: Winnow using a traditional basket or mechanical blower
- Purpose: Remove dust, foreign matter, stems, and debris
- This step directly affects the "foreign matter ≤5%" grading criterion

--- 4.6 STORAGE ---
- Store in moisture-proof containers away from direct sunlight
- Ensure cloves are FULLY dry before storing — any moisture causes rot
- Storage room: clean, dry, cool, and pest-free
- Mosquito netting on windows to prevent insect entry
- Do NOT store near strong-smelling items (detergents, paints, other foods)
  — these contaminate clove aroma and flavor
- If cloves absorb moisture in storage: re-dry to 10% moisture before sale
- Inspect stored cloves regularly for signs of spoilage or moisture

--- 4.7 PACKAGING ---
- Package in sealed polythene bags of various sizes per market demand
- Seal bags to prevent moisture ingress (use sealing machines)
- Apply labels with: product name, manufacturer details, date of manufacture,
  expiry date, weight, and any other required country-specific information

SECTION 5: DISEASE MANAGEMENT
================================================================

--- 5.1 CLOVE SUDDEN DEATH DISEASE (Ugonjwa wa Kifo cha Ghafla) ---
THREAT LEVEL: Critical — most existential disease in Zanzibar/Pemba

Causative Agent: Valsa eugeniae (also called Cytospora eugeniae)
Type: Fungal disease (ugonjwa wa fangasi)

Symptoms (Dalili):
1. Leaf fall / defoliation (majani ya karafuu kupukutika)
2. Wilting leaves remaining on tree turn rust/brownish color
3. Root rot (kuoza kwa mizizi)
4. Inner stem tissue turns yellow under outer bark
5. Yellowing of leaves due to loss of chlorophyll

How it spreads:
- Primarily by rainwater (carries spores from roots of infected tree to neighboring trees)
- Begins in rainy season but can persist into dry season
- Waterlogged soil accelerates root rot and spread

Why trees die:
- Pathogen causes root rot → tree cannot transport enough water → death

Control / Treatment:
- Spray COPPER OXY CHLORIDE every 14 days, especially during heavy rain season
- Early and consistent application prevents visible disease symptoms

Scale of impact in Zanzibar (2024–2025):
- Over 500,000 clove trees in Unguja North affected
- Significant loss to capital stock; trees take 5–7 years to replace

--- 5.2 CLOVE DIEBACK ---
Control:
- Apply 1% Bordeaux mixture to affected areas
- Remove/prune overcrowded or diseased branches to prevent spread

--- 5.3 CLOVE LEAF SPOT (Ugonjwa wa Madoa Jani) ---
Causative Agent: Colletotrichum spp. or Gloeosporium spp.
Type: Fungal disease

Symptoms:
- Black spots on clove leaves (madoa meusi kwenye majani)
- Leaf damage reduces overall tree health and yield

Control:
- Apply Prophylactic + Bordeaux mixture combined in equal 50/50 ratio
- Available at agrochemical shops (Agrovet) or consult your local agricultural extension officer

--- 5.4 CLOVE STEM BORER (Dumuzi / Kipekecha Shuna) ---
Type: Insect pest

How it damages:
- Attacks the mature lower stem of the tree
- Lays eggs; larvae bore through outer bark into the core stem
- Reduces productivity and can kill trees if untreated long-term

Control:
- Keep farm clean — remove weeds, rotting logs (breeding grounds)
- Spray insecticides: Fenthion or Cypermethrin
- For active borer holes: apply 2–4 ml of kerosene/paraffin into the hole to kill the larva

--- 5.5 SCALE INSECTS (Mdudu Gamba / Insects Scale) ---
Type: Insect pest

How it damages:
- Lives under leaves or on young stems
- Sucks plant sap → leaves curl, turn yellow, and drop
- Stunted growth of young leaves
- Produces black sooty mold that blocks photosynthesis

Control:
- Apply insecticides containing: Dimethoate, Acephate, Bifenthrin, Malathion,
  Permethrin, Imidacloprid, or Thiamethoxam
- Avoid overcrowding trees
- Prune to open up dense canopy that favors scale infestation

SECTION 6: MARKET AND ECONOMIC CONTEXT (ZANZIBAR)
================================================================

--- 6.1 ZSTC ROLE ---
- ZSTC (Zanzibar State Trading Corporation) is the government entity
  that buys cloves from all farmers and exports them abroad
- ZSTC holds a legal monopsony: farmers are required to sell to ZSTC
- ZSTC sets the farmgate price and manages all exports

--- 6.2 ZSTC 2023 OFFICIAL BUYING PRICES ---
Grade 1: TZS 15,000/kg
Grade 2: TZS 13,500/kg (-10% vs Grade 1)
Grade 3: TZS 12,500/kg (-16.7% vs Grade 1)
Grade 4 (Mpeta): TZS 7,000/kg (-53.3% vs Grade 1)

--- 6.3 INDUSTRY CHALLENGES (2024–2025) ---
1. Sudden Death disease destroyed 500,000+ trees in Unguja North
2. Erratic rainfall (climate change) caused premature flower drop and
   poor drying conditions, downgrading cloves to Grade 3/Mpeta
3. Aging tree stock (many trees planted in 1960s–70s) reducing yield
4. Export volume fell 69% to 1,200 tonnes (from 3,900 tonnes prior year)
5. Export value fell 76% to $6.3 million (from $28.8 million prior year)
6. Global market: Madagascar had 50–70% production shortfall, meaning
   genuine global demand exists — Zanzibar's problem is supply-side

--- 6.4 GLOBAL CONTEXT ---
- Indonesia: Largest producer (~70% of global supply) but consumes
  85–90% domestically (Kretek cigarettes). Imports from East Africa.
- Madagascar: Largest exporter; suffered 50–70% crop loss in 2024–25
- Zanzibar + Pemba: Historically significant exporters
- Global prices for premium cloves remain high ($6.8–$8.4/kg) —
  well above ZSTC's implied unit value of $5.25/kg in 2025, suggesting
  quality or structural pricing issues
